## Field result

The current project should be routed as a durable part of Coonie’s personal and technical history, not as a detached run of image-generation or packaging tasks. A primary public record from 2019 shows the same governing proposition now realized more elaborately in 2026: aesthetics and software compatibility are coequal criteria for choosing and inhabiting a computing environment, while resource thrift is deliberately secondary on capable hardware.

The 2019 record also establishes that Coonie’s Linux desktop was already an ecology rather than a single theme. It combined:

- DarkCold GTK;
- a separately distributed DarkCold Cinnamon component;
- CinnVIIStarkMenu;
- Crystal Diamond icons with another theme inserted as the first inheritance fallback for missing coverage;
- a separately installed cursor made global through icon-theme inheritance;
- Belligerent Madness plus CSS repairs;
- a raccoon menu-button image;
- and a chosen mid-2000s Mountain Dew wallpaper.

Coonie explicitly called his taste mid-2000s or “2005.” Three months earlier he had called Vista the best-looking OS, preferred XP’s long Start button, and named Classic Shell and WindowBlinds as earlier customization infrastructure.

## Why the 2026 work is historically new without being historically discontinuous

The new capability is maintenance resolution. The project now distinguishes and tests GTK widgets, Muffin/Metacity decorations, Cinnamon shell/applications, icon inheritance and sizing, symbolic status-icon behavior, Xcursor production, typography, and application-specific CSS.

The current working doctrine is best summarized as **compatibility archaeology**:

> keep the old machinery operational and personally alive; repair version drift at the owning layer; do not modernize away the dimensionality, color, pictoriality, or weirdness that made the machinery worth preserving.

This doctrine arose from concrete failures:

- SlickCold’s GTK-side modernization did not supply an equivalent Cinnamon companion and its decorations failed under Muffin badly enough to remove buttons and window dragging.
- DarkCold maintenance has a named “tiny black coffin” status-area regression.
- recoloring lost multicolor gradient glow, titlebar color, and purple control accents by drifting back toward generic blue.
- Cinnamon’s panel applets continued requesting symbolic battery/Wi-Fi/etc. assets unless explicitly overridden.
- the Y2K/Aero icon room produced folders that were too flat relative to Nova7 and encountered implausibly huge icons in Nemo on Linux Mint 21.3.

These are useful negative constraints for later archive retrieval and future package audits.

## Stable aesthetic constraints worth routing forward

- “Retro” means tactile mid-2000s software objects, not beige nostalgia and not modern flatness with an old color palette.
- Vista glass and dimensional richness can coexist with XP-like structural preferences.
- Colorfulness and character outrank perfect cohesion.
- Aqua/sea-green, blue, purple, pink, chocolate, cream, and near-black form a recurring personal palette; purple/multicolor states must not silently become generic blue.
- Gradient glow, bevels, pictorial folders, rendered status objects, and distinctive buttons are semantic parts of the intended visual language.
- Coonie strongly dislikes symbolic icons and prefers actual images with character.
- Belligerent Madness is not an incidental specimen; it recurs from the 2019 desktop into current theme and userstyle work.
- Reference screenshots and earlier working packages outrank generic style labels.
